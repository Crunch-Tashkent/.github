### Overview
The EU-Central Asia summit held on April 4, 2025, in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, marked a significant step in fostering cooperation between the European Union and the five Central Asian countries: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. This event brought together high-level leaders to discuss strategic partnerships, economic opportunities, and shared challenges.



#### Key Attendees and Speakers
The summit saw participation from Central Asian presidents, including Shavkat Mirziyoyev of Uzbekistan (the host), Kassym-Jomart Tokayev of Kazakhstan, Sadyr Japarov of Kyrgyzstan, Serdar Berdimuhamedov of Turkmenistan, and Emomali Rahmon of Tajikistan. From the EU, key attendees included António Costa, President of the European Council, and Ursula von der Leyen, President of the European Commission, along with Odile Renaud-Basso of the EBRD and leaders from the European Investment Bank.

Speakers like Ursula von der Leyen highlighted the strategic importance of Central Asia for trade and investment, announcing a €12 billion package. António Costa focused on security issues, while Shavkat Mirziyoyev emphasized regional cooperation and international law.

#### Economic Initiatives and Investors
The EU committed a €12 billion investment, with allocations for transport (€3 billion), critical raw materials (€2.5 billion), water, energy, and climate (€6.4 billion), and digital connectivity. The EBRD and European Investment Bank played key roles in supporting these initiatives, with plans for an Investors Forum later in 2025 to boost the Trans-Caspian Transport Corridor.

#### Context and Implications
The summit occurred amid global economic tensions, following US trade tariff announcements, and coincided with the Samarkand Climate Forum. Human rights concerns were raised, and the Khujand Declaration underscored regional unity among some Central Asian nations.

For more details, see [Euronews report](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/04/eu-seals-new-central-asia-partnership-deal-as-debut-samarkand-summit-ends).

### Survey Note: Comprehensive Analysis of the EU-Central Asia Summit, Samarkand, April 4, 2025

The first EU-Central Asia summit, convened on April 4, 2025, in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, represents a landmark event in the diplomatic and economic relations between the European Union and the Central Asian region, comprising Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. This summit, hosted by Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev, aimed to enhance strategic partnerships, address common challenges, and explore new avenues for cooperation. The following analysis provides a detailed examination of the key attendees, speakers, investors, and contextual factors, drawing from multiple credible sources published around the event date, with a focus on the information available as of April 10, 2025.

#### Event Background and Significance
The summit, postponed from 2024 to 2025 due to the formation of a new EU government, was the first high-level meeting of its kind, signaling a political upgrade in EU-Central Asia relations. It occurred in a turbulent geopolitical and economic climate, notably following US President Donald Trump’s announcement of global trade tariffs on April 3, 2025, which rattled markets and drew criticism from world leaders ([Euronews report](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/04/eu-seals-new-central-asia-partnership-deal-as-debut-samarkand-summit-ends)). This context underscored the urgency of diversifying trade and investment partnerships for both regions.

The event also coincided with the Samarkand Climate Forum on April 4–5, 2025, highlighting Europe’s interest in aligning diplomatic dialogue with sustainability goals ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/04/next-level-for-eu-and-central-asia-ties-heres-what-to-expect-from-samarkand-summit-this-week/)). Additionally, the Khujand Declaration, signed on March 29–30, 2025, by the presidents of Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Uzbekistan, marked a significant step toward regional unity and cooperation, setting the stage for the summit ([Euronews unity report](https://www.euronews.com/2025/04/04/central-asia-eu-summit-in-samarkand-marks-historic-regional-unity)).

#### Key Attendees
The summit saw robust participation from Central Asian leadership, with the following presidents in attendance:
- Shavkat Mirziyoyev of Uzbekistan, serving as the host and opening the summit.
- Kassym-Jomart Tokayev of Kazakhstan, a key figure in regional trade discussions.
- Sadyr Japarov of Kyrgyzstan, emphasizing regional stability.
- Serdar Berdimuhamedov of Turkmenistan, focusing on energy cooperation.
- Emomali Rahmon of Tajikistan, involved in water and climate initiatives.

From the EU, the delegation included:
- António Costa, President of the European Council, leading discussions on security and international law.
- Ursula von der Leyen, President of the European Commission, driving economic and investment agendas.
- Odile Renaud-Basso, President of the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD), underscoring financial support.
- Leadership of the European Investment Bank, focusing on infrastructure projects.

Preparatory efforts were noted, with Saida Mirziyoyeva, Assistant to the President of Uzbekistan, involved in bilateral preparations, though not a key summit attendee ([Gazeta.uz announcement](https://www.gazeta.uz/en/2025/01/23/summit/)).

#### Key Speakers and Their Contributions
The summit featured significant speeches that shaped its outcomes:
- **Ursula von der Leyen** outlined the potential benefits of stronger ties, emphasizing Central Asia’s strategic location for global trade routes and investment flows. She announced a €12 billion investment package under the EU’s Global Gateway Initiative, with detailed allocations: €3 billion for transport, €2.5 billion for critical raw materials, €6.4 billion for water, energy, and climate, and unspecified amounts for digital connectivity ([Euronews reliability pitch](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/04/unlike-others-were-reliable-eu-tells-central-asia-as-it-seeks-greater-access-to-rare-earth)). This package aims to enhance economic ties and support sustainable development.
- **António Costa** addressed common security challenges, including terrorism, violent extremism, and drug trafficking, and stressed the importance of international law, particularly in the context of the Ukraine situation. His remarks underscored the EU’s commitment to peaceful conflict resolution ([EEAS summit details](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/uzbekistan/eu-central-asia-summit-samarkand-uzbekistan-4-april-2025_en)).
- **Shavkat Mirziyoyev** emphasized Uzbekistan’s adherence to international law and welcomed negotiation processes for peaceful settlements, such as in Ukraine. He also praised the improvement of neighborly relations in Central Asia, noting that such summits would have been unimaginable a short time ago, reflecting regional progress ([Euronews unity report](https://www.euronews.com/2025/04/04/central-asia-eu-summit-in-samarkand-marks-historic-regional-unity)).

These speeches collectively framed the summit as a platform for both economic and geopolitical alignment, with a focus on mutual benefits and shared responsibilities.

#### Key Investors and Economic Initiatives
The economic dimension of the summit was robust, with significant investor involvement and initiatives:
- **European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD):** The EBRD’s presence, led by Odile Renaud-Basso, underscored the EU’s commitment to economic development. The bank is expected to facilitate investments and support reforms, with plans to establish a local office in Uzbekistan to enhance operational efficiency ([Euronews reliability pitch](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/04/unlike-others-were-reliable-eu-tells-central-asia-as-it-seeks-greater-access-to-rare-earth)).
- **European Investment Bank:** The leadership attended to leverage financial resources for infrastructure and development projects, aligning with the EU’s broader investment strategy ([EEAS summit details](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/uzbekistan/eu-central-asia-summit-samarkand-uzbekistan-4-april-2025_en)).
- **EU’s €12 Billion Investment Package:** This package, announced by von der Leyen, is a cornerstone of the summit’s economic outcomes, with specific allocations:
  - Transport: €3 billion, focusing on enhancing connectivity, particularly through the Trans-Caspian Transport Corridor.
  - Critical Raw Materials: €2.5 billion, aiming to secure access to resources vital for green transitions.
  - Water, Energy, and Climate: €6.4 billion, addressing sustainability and climate goals, aligning with the Samarkand Climate Forum.
  - Digital Connectivity: Included, though exact figures were not specified, to boost technological cooperation.

- **Investors Forum:** An agreement was reached to hold an Investors Forum later in 2025, specifically targeting the Trans-Caspian Transport Corridor (Middle Corridor). This initiative aims to reduce export times between the EU and Central Asia by half, bypassing Russia, and is expected to attract further investments ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/04/next-level-for-eu-and-central-asia-ties-heres-what-to-expect-from-samarkand-summit-this-week/)).

Additional economic context includes the EU accounting for 22.6% of Central Asia’s combined foreign trade in 2023, with over 40% of investments in the region coming from the EU, particularly notable in Kazakhstan with $10.3 billion in foreign direct investment (FDI) in 2023 and a total of $200 billion since 2005, supported by over 3,000 European companies operating there ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/04/next-level-for-eu-and-central-asia-ties-heres-what-to-expect-from-samarkand-summit-this-week/)).

#### Human Rights and Broader Context
Ahead of the summit, human rights organizations urged the EU to address human rights issues in Central Asia, emphasizing the importance of sustainable partnerships that respect the rule of law and human rights. This call was particularly relevant given the region’s historical challenges, and it seems likely that these concerns were part of the dialogue, though specific outcomes are still emerging ([Euronews report](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/04/eu-seals-new-central-asia-partnership-deal-as-debut-samarkand-summit-ends)).

The geopolitical context was further complicated by the timing, occurring a day after Trump’s tariff announcements, which added urgency to the EU’s efforts to deepen ties with Central Asia as a counterbalance to potential trade disruptions. The summit’s alignment with the Samarkand Climate Forum also reflects Europe’s broader strategy to integrate sustainability into diplomatic engagements, with the Khujand Declaration reinforcing regional cooperation efforts ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/04/next-level-for-eu-and-central-asia-ties-heres-what-to-expect-from-samarkand-summit-this-week/)).

#### Conclusion
The EU-Central Asia summit on April 4, 2025, in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, appears to have been a pivotal moment for enhancing bilateral and regional cooperation. With key leaders from both regions in attendance, significant economic commitments like the €12 billion package, and a focus on security and sustainability, the summit laid groundwork for deeper ties. However, ongoing discussions around human rights and the geopolitical implications of global trade tensions suggest that the full impact is still unfolding. This analysis, based on reports up to April 10, 2025, provides a comprehensive view, acknowledging the complexity and potential for future developments.
